Occasional Prayers

John Donne

Prayers of John Donne (1572–1631), English poet and dean of St Paul’s Cathedral

  • A Confession John Donne
  • Bring us, O Lord John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer I — Insultus morbi primus (“The first assault of the illness”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer II — Post actio laesa (“After the action is impaired”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer III — Decubitus sequitur tandem (“Finally, he lies down”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer IV — Medicusque vocatur (“And the doctor is called”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer V — Solus adest (“He is alone”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer VI — Metuit (“He fears”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer VII — Socios sibi jungier instat (“He insists on joining companions”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer VIII — Et rex ipse suum mittit (“And even the king sends his own”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer IX — Medicamina scribunt (“They prescribe medicines”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer X — Lente et serpenti satagunt occurre re morbo (“They strive to meet the creeping disease”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XI — Nobilibusque trahunt, a cincto corde, venenum, succis et gemmis, et qua (“And with noble drugs, they keep the venom and malignity from the heart”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XII — Spirante columba supposita pedibus, revocantur ad ima vapores (“With the breath of a dove placed under the feet, vapors are recalled to the depths”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XIII — Ingeniumque malum, numeroso stigmate, fassus pellitur ad pectus, morb (“And the evil nature, declared with numerous scars, is driven to the chest”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XIV — Idque notant criticis medici evenisse diebus (“And the doctors note that this happened on critical days”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XV — Interea insomnes noctes ego duco, diesque (“Meanwhile, I spend sleepless nights and days”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XVI — Et properare meum clamant, e turre propinqua, obstreperae campanae ali (“And the noisy bells from the nearby tower urge me to hurry”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XVII — Nunc lento sonitu dicunt, morieris (“Now with a slow sound, they say, ‘you will die’”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XVIII — At inde mortuus es, sonitu celeri, pulsuque agitato (“But from that, ‘you are dead’, they say with a rapid sound and agitated beat”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XIX — Oceano tandem emenso, aspicienda resurgit terra; vident, justis, medic (“Finally, after crossing the ocean, land to be seen arises; the just, doctors see”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XX — Id agunt (“They do this”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XXI — Atque annuit ille, qui, per eos, clamat, linquas jam, lazare, lectum (“And he consents, who, through them, cries out, ‘Lazarus, now leave your bed’”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XXII — Sit morbi fomes tibi cura (“Let the cause of the illness be a concern to you”) John Donne
  • Devotions, Prayer XXIII — Metusque, relabi (“And the fear of relapsing”) John Donne
